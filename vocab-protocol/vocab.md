# Vocabulary

A starter perceptual vocabulary. Ten qualities, chosen because they cover distinct aesthetic territory, are immediately recognisable to designers, and produce different agent output when asked for more or less of them.

This file is intended to be included in a project alongside `mood.md`. An agent reading both knows not just what the work should be about, but what it should feel like in its bones.

Format spec: see `FORMAT.md`. To extend the vocabulary for your project, see `PROMPT.md`.

---

## warmth

**One line:** Emotional temperature; the quality of welcome in a thing.

**When it applies:** When a product needs to feel like a friend rather than an institution. When the gesture should soften the user rather than instruct them. Warmth is generous, slow to interrupt, comfortable with imperfection.

**References:**
- Substack's reader mode — serif typography, generous line height, no rush
- A handwritten letter on good paper — the trace of a person, the slight wobble of the line
- The interior of a good independent bookshop — light, smell, the geometry of disordered abundance

**Anti-references:**
- A government tax form — built for compliance, correctly indifferent to comfort
- A hotel chain lobby — neutral by design, refuses to welcome any one person in particular
- Most enterprise SaaS dashboards — engineered for power, not greeting

**Pairs well with:** intimacy, materiality
**Tensions with:** precision (when precision becomes cold), authority (when authority becomes distant)

---

## density

**One line:** Visual and informational compression; how much sits in a given amount of space.

**When it applies:** When the work earns its presence through volume of decision rather than spaciousness. When the user is a power user who wants the surface to honour their attention. When sparseness would feel patronising.

**References:**
- Bloomberg Terminal — every pixel earns its place, the interface refuses to flatter you
- A page from the Whole Earth Catalog — text, image, marginalia, all coexisting
- A spreadsheet built by someone who genuinely knows their domain

**Anti-references:**
- A modern landing page with three paragraphs per scroll
- A consumer meditation app — calm by design, but starved of substance
- A presentation slide with one giant headline and a stock image

**Pairs well with:** precision, authority
**Tensions with:** warmth, intimacy

---

## restraint

**One line:** The quality of what is deliberately not shown.

**When it applies:** When the work is more powerful for its omissions. When silence does the work the speaker can't. Restraint is the second draft, not the first. It knows what to delete.

**References:**
- Apple's product pages circa 2010 — one image, one sentence, nothing else
- A Dieter Rams calculator — every element answers a question, none of them ask one
- A Muji store — the absence of branding is the branding

**Anti-references:**
- A SaaS dashboard trying to show every metric on every screen
- A festival poster trying to list every act in the lineup
- A homepage hero making four promises before you've scrolled

**Pairs well with:** precision, authority
**Tensions with:** density (when density wants more), irreverence (when irreverence wants louder)

---

## irreverence

**One line:** Willingness to break conventions; levity in the work.

**When it applies:** When the audience is in on the joke. When the seriousness of the category needs a hand on its shoulder. Irreverence is not chaos — it knows the rules well enough to break them on purpose.

**References:**
- The Oatmeal's interface — handmade, ridiculous, completely consistent with itself
- A Wes Anderson title sequence — formal in its irreverence
- The early Tumblr UI — the platform took its own seriousness as a category of joke

**Anti-references:**
- A government tax form (rightly so)
- A funeral home website (also rightly so)
- A medical compliance notice (correctly grave)

**Pairs well with:** warmth, intimacy
**Tensions with:** authority, restraint

---

## materiality

**One line:** The sense of stuff — texture, physical presence, the trace of the made thing.

**When it applies:** When digital work needs to feel like a real thing was touched. When the interface should hold the trace of its maker. Materiality is the opposite of the floating, frictionless plane.

**References:**
- A letterpress poster — the ink sits inside the paper, not on it
- Aesop's product photography — light, surface, the weight of a bottle in a hand
- A vinyl record sleeve in its original printing

**Anti-references:**
- Flat design pushed to its purest extreme — no grain, no shadow, no weight
- A standard PowerPoint template — history-free, place-free
- A SaaS marketing page using generic 3D illustration

**Pairs well with:** warmth, intimacy, friction
**Tensions with:** precision (when precision flattens)

---

## precision

**One line:** Exactness of intent; the sharpness of decision visible in the work.

**When it applies:** When the work needs to communicate competence above all. When the user is trusting the surface with money, health, or identity. Precision is not coldness — it is care expressed as exactness.

**References:**
- Linear's interface — every interaction has been thought about twice
- A Swiss train schedule — information designed to be acted upon, not admired
- The key travel on a good keyboard — engineered to the millimetre

**Anti-references:**
- A blog template described as "responsive, clean, modern" without specifics
- A landing page that's "fun" when its job is "trustworthy"
- A hand-drawn web typography choice when the context calls for clarity

**Pairs well with:** authority, density, restraint
**Tensions with:** irreverence, materiality (when materiality becomes mess)

---

## authority

**One line:** Confidence and gravitas; how settled the voice of the work is.

**When it applies:** When the work needs to be believed. When the topic is serious and tentativeness would undermine the claim. Authority is not aggression. It is the quality of having earned the right to speak.

**References:**
- A Penguin Classics cover — the typography says read me with no exclamation
- The New York Times masthead — the same since 1967, and the certainty matters
- The gov.uk design system — quiet, complete, trusted

**Anti-references:**
- A startup pitch deck with five exclamation points in the hero
- A B2B marketing page using "amazing!" three times before the fold
- A press release written like a tweet

**Pairs well with:** precision, restraint, density
**Tensions with:** irreverence, intimacy (when intimacy becomes small talk)

---

## intimacy

**One line:** Closeness, vulnerability, the small-scale care of one person talking to one person.

**When it applies:** When the work happens between two people, not between an institution and a crowd. When the second person should feel addressed by name. Intimacy is the writer who knows you.

**References:**
- A good Substack newsletter — the voice is in the room with you
- Field Notes notebooks — pocketable, hand-feel, slightly imperfect
- A margin note in a borrowed book

**Anti-references:**
- A safety announcement at an airport
- A corporate "Our Story" page that uses we but means the brand team
- An enterprise software splash screen

**Pairs well with:** warmth, materiality, friction
**Tensions with:** authority, density

---

## friction

**One line:** Productive resistance; the texture of slowness made on purpose.

**When it applies:** When the work should slow the user down rather than speed them through. When the decision deserves attention. Friction is not failure — it is the design choice to make something feel like a thing, not a glide.

**References:**
- A vinyl record player — the sequence of place needle, lower arm, listen is the point
- Are.na's intentional pacing — the platform refuses to be a feed
- A bound paper journal you have to open with two hands

**Anti-references:**
- An infinite scroll feed
- A one-click checkout — a virtue in its context, the opposite of friction in this one
- An autoplaying video carousel

**Pairs well with:** materiality, intimacy, restraint
**Tensions with:** precision (when precision means frictionless)

---

## rhythm

**One line:** Pacing, repetition, the cadence of elements as the eye moves through.

**When it applies:** When a product is composed in time, not just in space. When the user reads, scrolls, or progresses through the work. Rhythm is the music of layout.

**References:**
- A well-paced long-form essay — paragraphs of varying length, the pull quote landing where the breath would
- A magazine spread from The Gentlewoman — image, text, white, image
- A Wes Anderson set composition — symmetry made into a beat

**Anti-references:**
- A wall of bullet points — no rhythm, just stacking
- A landing page that goes hero, hero, hero with no breath
- A spreadsheet without column groupings or visual rests

**Pairs well with:** density, restraint, intimacy
**Tensions with:** chaos (which would be a useful term in some vocabularies — not in this one)

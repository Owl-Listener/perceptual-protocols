# Mood: Char & Ember

> **Artifact note:** This is the verbatim Step 1 output from the Char & Ember experiment — the brief that mood-protocol extracted from a Pinterest moodboard of off-grid cabin references. Produced by Claude Opus 4.7 in Claude Code, May 2026.

## Essence

A small dark box set against a cold wild landscape, with a single orange fire burning inside it. This is the perceptual identity of the off-grid hut: a charred-timber or rust-metal shell, a raw-plywood warm interior, one saturated ember of heat and light, and the whole thing earning its meaning by standing alone in moorland, pine forest, or by a loch in failing light. The feeling to design for is *arrival at dusk* — you see the stove-glow through the window before you reach the door. Make things that feel sheltered, weathered, hand-made, and warm in spite of the cold around them. Restraint everywhere except the one hot point.

## Perceptual Vocabulary

*This experiment requires anchoring to the canonical vocabulary (`vocab-protocol/vocab.md`) and proposing new terms only where it falls short.*

**Canonical anchors that genuinely fit:**

- **`materiality`** (primary) — the dominant quality. Charred larch, rusted Corten, raw birch ply, cast iron, wool kilim, clay tile. Every surface holds the trace of being a made, touched, weathered thing.
- **`warmth`** (primary) — the orange enamel stove is the recurring hero object across both boards. This is hearth-warmth: generous, fire-lit, the opposite of an institution.
- **`restraint`** (primary) — every building is one confident gesture. A single monopitch, a single gable, no decoration. Scandinavian reduction, not maximalism.
- **`intimacy`** (primary) — scale is for one or two people. The cabin for the self, the solo retreat, the chair-by-the-fire. Closeness, not crowd.
- **`friction`** (supporting) — off-grid is deliberate slowness made physical: chop the wood, light the stove, no glide. The work should feel like a thing, not a feed.
- **`rhythm`** (supporting) — the repeated simple silhouette across the grid (gable, monopitch, lit window) is a visual beat. Sameness made into cadence.

**Canonical term held in tension (do not resolve toward the cold side):**

- **`precision`** — the architecture is exactingly detailed (clean Scandinavian craft, sharp monopitch geometry) but it is *precision in service of warmth*, never the cold Linear/Swiss-schedule kind. Keep the craft; refuse the chill.

**Proposed new terms (canonical set has no equivalent):**

- **`refuge`** — *the relief of being enclosed while the wild stays outside; the prospect-refuge feeling of the lit hut in the dark.* This is the soul of the entire board and nothing in the canonical ten names it. `warmth` is about welcome; `intimacy` is about scale; neither is about *protection against an exterior threat*. Propose adding to project vocabulary. Pairs with: warmth, materiality. Tensions with: density.
- **`patina`** — *the beauty of a surface aged in the open: char, silvering, rust, oxidation embraced as finish, not failure.* Distinct from `materiality` (touch/presence) — this is specifically *time and exposure acting on a surface as a design choice*. Pairs with: materiality, friction. Tensions with: precision (when precision means pristine).
- **`ember`** — *a single saturated warm point-source glowing inside an otherwise cool, dark field.* Both a colour fact and a composition rule. It is what gives the board its electricity. An intensification of `warmth` specific enough to earn its own name and to drive the colour and layout sections below.

**Explicitly NOT (canonical anti-anchors):** `density` (these are spare, not compressed) and `irreverence` (this is earnest; nothing here winks).

## Colour

- **Temperature:** A warm core held inside a cool envelope. Fire and wood at the centre; highland, forest, dusk, mist around it. The temperature *tension* is the mood — never let it resolve fully warm or fully cool.
- **Palette:**
  - `#1C1A17` "charred larch" — warm near-black; the shell, never pure #000
  - `#C8531F` "stove ember" — the one hot point: enamel stove, Corten, fire-glow
  - `#8A3A1C` "weathered corten" — deeper rust, structural warm
  - `#7C3A2E` "falu red" — Swedish barn red, the painted-timber note
  - `#E0C49B` "raw birch ply" — blonde interior timber, the warm light surface
  - `#9A9082` "silvered larch" — aged grey-brown weathered cladding
  - `#A07A45` "bracken gold" — autumn moorland ground
  - `#4A4F3E` "highland moss" — muted cool green, the wild
  - `#3E4A42` "forest dusk" — deep cool green-grey, the loch/pine
  - `#E6E1D6` "lime plaster" — warm off-white interior wall / misted sky
- **Contrast:** High, but never graphic. Char-black against ember-orange against blonde ply — not black against white. The contrast is *temperature and material*, not luminance alone.
- **Saturation:** Overwhelmingly muted, desaturated earth — *with one or two highly saturated ember/rust accents doing all the chromatic work.* Discipline: one hot colour, everything else dialled down to bracken, moss, char, plaster.

## Typography

*(Inferred — the board is architecture, not type. These choices serve the feeling.)*

- **Character:** Settled, sturdy, lightly weathered. The typographic register of a good Scandinavian architecture monograph or a *Cabin Porn* / *Kinfolk* book — quiet editorial confidence, not statement. Honest, not decorative; calm, not loud.
- **Weight distribution:** Hierarchy from a narrow band — regular and medium carrying most of it, one heavier cut reserved for the single focal moment (the typographic equivalent of the ember). No hairline-thin display weights; nothing here is fragile.
- **Density:** Airy. Generous line height and margin. The page should breathe like the buildings sit in the landscape.
- **Suggested pairings:** A warm old-style or transitional serif (Lyon, Tiempos Text, a softened Caslon) for reading, paired with a humanist grotesque (Söhne, Untitled Sans, National 2) for interface. Avoid geometric-cold sans (Futura, clinical Helvetica) and anything cute or hand-drawn.

## Space & Layout

- **Rhythm:** Generous negative space punctuated by a single warm focal point — the *ember principle applied to layout*. Large calm voids, then one hot thing your eye goes to. Horizon-anchored: things sit on a baseline the way a cabin sits on its site.
- **Grid character:** Quiet and regular, with deliberate asymmetric energy — the monopitch lean, the off-centre window, the long low horizontal. Ordered but not symmetrical; never rigid, never broken-for-effect.
- **Scale relationships:** Small object, big context. The designed thing should feel modest and precise against a much larger, cooler, emptier surround. Don't fill the frame — let the landscape (whitespace) make the shelter matter.

## Texture & Material

- **Surface quality:** Tactile and layered. Visible wood grain, char texture, the orange-peel of stove enamel, rust bloom, wool weave. Nothing floats; everything has weight and a maker's trace.
- **Material references:** Charred and silvered timber, raw birch/CLT plywood, Corten and blackened steel, cast iron, terracotta and clay tile, wool kilim, firewood, lime plaster, single-pane glass with a warm room behind it.
- **Finish:** Matte, every time. The only glow permitted is *light* (fire, a lit window at dusk) — never gloss, never gradient sheen, never plastic. Patina welcome; polish suspect.

## Emotional Register

You have walked a long way in cold, failing light. The cabin is small and dark against the hill, and the stove is already lit — you can see the orange through the window before you reach the door. Inside: raw wood, a low chair, the smell of woodsmoke, one warm circle of light and everything else softened to the edge of dark. This is solitude that was *chosen*, not loneliness — refuge, not isolation. The register is earned warmth: comfort that means more because of the cold it's set against. Think Cabin Porn, Scandinavian off-grid, Highland bothy, the last hour of an autumn afternoon.

## Design Principles

- **One hot point** — A single saturated ember against an earthy field. All chromatic and attentional energy goes to one place; everything else is restrained to make it land.
- **Dark shell, warm core** — A cool or blackened envelope holding a warm, lit interior. The contrast between the two *is* the entire idea — design for the threshold between them.
- **Honest, weathered materials** — Show grain, char, rust, and age as finish. Never simulate a material, never gloss it, never hide the trace of time. Patina is the point.
- **One clear gesture** — A single confident form and nothing decorative. If an element doesn't carry structural or emotional load, delete it. Scandinavian reduction, not feature accretion.
- **Set against the wild** — The work only means something in tension with something colder, bigger, and untamed. Always keep the context that makes the shelter feel like shelter; never present it floating, placeless, or studio-lit.

## Anti-References

No anti-reference files were supplied; these are inferred, plus the noise the board itself contains:

- **The sponsored commerce pins on the board** (Castlery beige showroom sofa; the generic white boutique-hotel living room; OMHU "Teddy Cream" boho interior). These are studio-lit, placeless, for-sale, history-free — the *exact opposite* of off-grid honesty. They are aesthetic noise injected by Pinterest; filter them out, do not absorb them. (The Claybrook terracotta herringbone tile is the one sponsored pin that genuinely reinforces clay `materiality` — keep that signal only.)
- **Glossy SaaS gradients, soft 3D illustration, frictionless flat design** — no grain, no weight, no place. Violates `materiality` and `patina`.
- **Bright, cheerful, multi-primary palettes** — breaks the "one hot point against muted earth" discipline. Colour everywhere = colour nowhere.
- **Cold minimalism** (empty white, clinical, Helvetica-neutral) — this mood is *spare*, never *sterile*; warm restraint, not absence.
- **Dense dashboards / information walls** — explicitly anti-`density`. This work is uncrowded by design.
- **Corporate stock photography, placeless interiors, anything "amazing!"** — earnest, not promotional; `irreverence` and hype are both wrong here.

## Agent Instructions

When generating UI, CSS, or design decisions under this mood: build a **cool, dark, restrained field and put exactly one warm saturated thing in it.** Background and chrome use `charred larch` (#1C1A17) or muted earth (`forest dusk`, `highland moss`, `lime plaster`); reserve `stove ember` (#C8531F) / `weathered corten` (#8A3A1C) for the single most important action or focal element per view — never as a general accent, never more than once or twice on screen. Surfaces are matte: no gradients, no glassmorphism, no drop-shadow gloss; suggest depth with warm material tone and grain, not sheen. Use real near-black (#1C1A17), never #000; real off-white (#E6E1D6), never #FFF — keep everything slightly warmed and slightly aged. Typography is a calm warm serif for reading + a humanist grotesque for UI, generous line height, airy spacing; one heavier weight is the typographic ember, used sparingly. Layout: lots of negative space (treat it as landscape), strong horizontal baseline, deliberate asymmetry (the monopitch lean), modest content set against large calm voids. Every element must justify its presence — if it doesn't carry structural or emotional load, cut it. Above all, design the *threshold*: the moment of moving from cold/dark/outside to warm/lit/inside is the experience. Make it feel like refuge, made of honest weathered material, with one fire burning.
